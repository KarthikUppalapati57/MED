# Data Processing Addendum

**Effective date:** July 24, 2026  
**Last updated:** July 24, 2026

This Data Processing Addendum (“DPA”) forms part of the agreement between Mindful Tech Solutions Inc., doing business as RestOps-360 (“RestOps-360,” “Processor,” “Service Provider,” “Contractor,” “we,” or “us”), and the customer entity that has entered into the Terms of Service or another written agreement for the Services (“Customer,” “Controller,” or “Business”).

This DPA applies to the extent we process Customer Personal Data on Customer’s behalf. Capitalized terms not defined in this DPA have the meanings in the applicable agreement (the “Agreement”) or Data Protection Laws.

## 1. Definitions

**Customer Personal Data** means personal data, personal information, or similar regulated information contained in Customer Data and processed by us on Customer’s behalf.

**Data Protection Laws** means privacy and data-protection laws applicable to the processing, including applicable U.S. state privacy laws and, where applicable, the EU General Data Protection Regulation, UK GDPR, and related national laws.

**Security Incident** means a confirmed breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to Customer Personal Data. It does not include unsuccessful attempts that do not compromise Customer Personal Data.

**Subprocessor** means a third party engaged by us to process Customer Personal Data for the Services.

## 2. Roles and Scope

Customer is the Controller or Business and RestOps-360 is the Processor, Service Provider, or Contractor, except when Customer acts as a processor for another controller, in which case we act as Customer’s subprocessor.

Customer determines the purposes and essential means of processing Customer Personal Data. We process Customer Personal Data only to provide, secure, support, and maintain the Services, perform the Agreement, follow Customer’s documented instructions, and comply with law.

The Agreement, Customer’s configuration and use of the Services, support requests, and written instructions consistent with the Agreement constitute documented instructions.

## 3. Customer Responsibilities

Customer will:

- comply with Data Protection Laws;
- provide required notices and obtain required rights, authorizations, and consents;
- submit only data appropriate for the Services;
- configure access, roles, integrations, retention, and security settings appropriately;
- respond to data-subject requests for Customer-controlled data; and
- avoid instructing us to process data unlawfully.

If we reasonably believe an instruction violates Data Protection Laws, we may suspend the affected processing and notify Customer, unless prohibited by law.

## 4. Processing Obligations

We will:

- process Customer Personal Data only on documented instructions and as required by law;
- inform Customer of a legally required processing instruction before processing, unless prohibited;
- ensure authorized personnel are subject to confidentiality obligations;
- limit access to personnel and service providers with a need to know;
- implement appropriate technical and organizational measures;
- reasonably assist Customer with data-subject requests, security assessments, impact assessments, and regulatory consultations, taking into account the nature of processing and information available to us; and
- maintain records and information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA.

## 5. U.S. State Privacy Terms

To the extent applicable under U.S. state privacy laws, RestOps-360 acts as Customer’s service provider or contractor and will:

- process personal information only for the limited and specified business purposes described in the Agreement and this DPA;
- not sell or share Customer Personal Data;
- not retain, use, or disclose Customer Personal Data outside the direct business relationship with Customer or for a commercial purpose other than providing the Services, except as permitted by law;
- not combine Customer Personal Data with personal information received from another person or collected from our own consumer interaction, except as permitted by law;
- provide the same level of privacy protection required by applicable law;
- notify Customer if we determine we can no longer meet applicable obligations; and
- allow Customer to take reasonable and appropriate steps to help ensure use is consistent with Customer’s obligations.

Customer may take reasonable steps to stop and remediate unauthorized use after notice.

## 6. Security

We maintain technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk, as summarized in Annex 3 and our public Security Overview.

Security measures are designed to protect Customer Personal Data against accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access, considering the state of the art, implementation cost, nature and scope of processing, and risks to individuals.

## 7. Security Incident Notification

We will notify Customer without undue delay after confirming a Security Incident affecting Customer Personal Data.

To the extent reasonably available, notice will describe:

- the nature of the Security Incident;
- the categories of affected data and individuals;
- likely consequences;
- measures taken or proposed; and
- a contact for follow-up.

We will take reasonable steps to contain, investigate, mitigate, and remediate the Security Incident and will reasonably cooperate with Customer’s legally required notifications. Notification is not an admission of fault or liability.

Customer is responsible for providing accurate security-notification contacts.

## 8. Subprocessors

Customer provides general authorization for us to engage the Subprocessors listed in Annex 2.

We will:

- impose data-protection and confidentiality obligations materially consistent with this DPA;
- remain responsible for the Subprocessor’s performance to the extent required by applicable law;
- update the Subprocessor list before a new Subprocessor begins processing Customer Personal Data; and
- provide reasonable notice of material new Subprocessors.

Customer may object on reasonable data-protection grounds within 15 days after notice. The parties will work in good faith to resolve the concern. If no commercially reasonable resolution is available, Customer may terminate the affected Service, and we will refund prepaid fees for the unused portion of the terminated Service.

## 9. Data Subject Requests

Taking into account the nature of processing, we will provide reasonable assistance to Customer in responding to verified requests to access, correct, delete, restrict, object, or obtain a copy of Customer Personal Data.

If we receive a request relating to Customer Personal Data, we may direct the requester to Customer and will not respond substantively except on Customer’s instruction or as required by law.

## 10. Deletion and Return

During the subscription, Customer may access or export Customer Data through available features.

After termination, we will make Customer Data available for export for a reasonable period, generally 30 days, and then delete Customer Personal Data from active systems, unless law requires retention or the parties agree otherwise.

Deletion from backups occurs through normal backup lifecycle processes. Any retained information remains protected and is processed only for the legally required purpose.

## 11. Audits and Information Rights

Upon reasonable written request, we will provide information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance, which may include current independent audit reports, certifications, summaries, questionnaires, or other appropriate materials when available.

If that information is insufficient, Customer may request an audit no more than once annually, unless required by a regulator or following a Security Incident. Audits must:

- occur during normal business hours with reasonable advance notice;
- avoid unreasonable disruption;
- protect other customers’ information and our confidential information;
- use an independent auditor bound by confidentiality; and
- be at Customer’s expense unless the audit identifies a material breach by us.

## 12. International Transfers

The Services are initially intended for U.S. customers and data processing.

Before Customer submits Customer Personal Data subject to EU, UK, Swiss, or other international transfer restrictions, the parties will ensure an appropriate transfer mechanism applies.

Where required and agreed:

- the European Commission Standard Contractual Clauses may be incorporated, with the module appropriate to the parties’ roles;
- the UK International Data Transfer Addendum may apply to UK transfers; and
- supplementary measures will be used where reasonably necessary.

Customer will identify affected data and transfer requirements before processing begins.

## 13. Liability and Order of Precedence

Liability under this DPA is subject to the liability provisions of the Agreement unless applicable law requires otherwise.

If there is a conflict concerning processing of Customer Personal Data, this DPA controls over the Agreement. Standard Contractual Clauses, if applicable, control over inconsistent terms as required by law.

## 14. Duration

This DPA remains effective while we process Customer Personal Data under the Agreement.

# Annex 3 — Technical and Organizational Measures

Our measures are designed to include, as appropriate to the Services and verified production configuration:

- role-based access control and organization, brand, location, and tenant scoping;
- database-level row security for supported data paths;
- least-privilege administrative access and restricted service credentials;
- password and session controls and optional multi-factor authentication;
- TLS encryption in transit;
- infrastructure-provider encryption at rest;
- separated and restricted handling of supported tax and banking secrets;
- logging of security-relevant access and administrative activity;
- secure development, code review, dependency review, and change control;
- vulnerability identification, prioritization, and remediation;
- backups, recovery capabilities, and continuity procedures;
- incident detection, escalation, containment, investigation, and notification;
- subprocessor diligence and contractual controls;
- personnel confidentiality and access removal; and
- customer-facing controls for roles, exports, deletion requests, and integrations.

Specific implementation details may be provided under confidentiality where appropriate.
